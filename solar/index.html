What happens when someone finds you

This is the part you never see, because it happens on their phone and they never make contact.

  1. They search, and you come up

    Maybe a Google listing, maybe a name a neighbour gave them. So far so good — this part is already working.

  2. They look for proof

    They want to see a system you've actually installed, roughly what it costs, and whether you work in their area. A phone number answers none of that.

  3. They don't phone a stranger

    Calling means committing to a conversation with someone who might be a chancer. It's easier to close the tab and look at the next installer, who has photos and a form.

One page, doing the whole job

Not a brochure with an "About Us". Everything a buyer needs in order to contact you with confidence, on one screen they can scroll on their phone.

  • What you actually fit

    Grid-tied, hybrid, off-grid, inverters, batteries, backup only. Stated plainly, so nobody phones you about work you don't do.

  • Photographs of finished jobs

    The single most persuasive thing you own. Send me what's on your phone — I'll crop, compress and lay them out.

  • Your service area

    Named towns and suburbs, which is also what people type into Google. Someone two provinces away stops wasting your time.

  • A quote form that emails you

    Roof type, current bill, backup or full solar. The enquiry arrives in your inbox already half-qualified, at eleven at night if that's when they were looking.

  • Phone and WhatsApp, one tap

    The number stays. It just stops being the only option, and it becomes tappable instead of something to write down.

  • Fast on a bad connection

    It loads in about a second on phone data. A site that takes eight seconds has already lost the person it was built for.
