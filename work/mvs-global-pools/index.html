Work / MVS Global Pools

A website for a pool company that never had one

MVS Global Pools is a pool company in Greenstone, Johannesburg, founded in 2014, with eleven staff working across South Africa. Until 29 August 2026 it had no website and no domain. I built it one, uninvited, and they bought it four days later. I still host and run it.

At a glance

The short version

Client: MVS Global Pools, founder Madodana Vodloza

Work: pool construction, renovation, leak repairs, pumps, heating, water features and weekly maintenance

Built: 26 August 2026. Live on 29 August, bought on 30 August

What I do now: hosting, DNS, email routing and changes, on a monthly

Built with: HTML, CSS and JavaScript on Cloudflare Pages, no build step

The problem

A national profile, and nothing to find

In February 2026, Scrolla.Africa ran an interview with the founder about building the company from nothing. Anyone who read it and searched for the business found a Facebook page. There was no website, and the domain had never been registered.

That gap was the whole pitch. The press had already done the hard part of earning trust, and the company had nowhere to send the people it earned.

Who it is for

Four visitors who want different things

A pool company's site gets four kinds of visitor. Someone pricing a new pool, the biggest job and the longest decision. Someone with a tired or leaking pool deciding between renovating and replacing. Someone whose pump needs fixing this week. And someone who wants weekly cleaning, which is the recurring work that keeps a pool company steady.

The page has one job: turn those visitors into quote requests for building and renovation, and turn some of them into maintenance contracts.

Decisions

What the page does, and why

The founder's story is proof, not decoration. A national publication had verified it, which is worth more than any "trusted since" badge. The page turns it into practical reasons to hire them: eleven people is enough hands to finish a build, and a company running since 2014 will still be there in year three when the pump goes.

They build and they service. Most pool companies do one or the other. MVS does both, so the page says plainly that there is nobody else to blame when something goes wrong. Their Facebook page had never said that.

Weekly maintenance gets its own section, aimed partly at holiday homes and rentals, where the owner usually finds out about a green pool from a guest.

No prices anywhere. Every pool is a different quote, so the FAQ explains why there is no price list instead of inventing a starting number.

Their own photographs. All 22 came from the business as WhatsApp exports. Re-saving them as JPEG made the files bigger, so they are WebP, 20% smaller with no visible change. The link preview image stays JPEG, because WhatsApp is how their customers share links and it handles WebP badly.

After launch

Set up so the business owns it

The domain is registered in the company's name, not mine. Enquiries go to an address on their own domain, with email authentication set up so it is not easily spoofed. The quote form goes straight to them. A separate review form lets customers leave a review, with an optional box to agree to it being published, because a name on a public page needs that permission.

I host it, keep it running and make the changes they ask for, on a monthly.
