Work / Kōhī by Ifuku

A café site that loads before the queue moves

Kōhī by Ifuku is a coffee shop, micro-gallery and jazz kissa (a listening room built around a hi-fi) at 44 Stanley in Milpark, Johannesburg. I rebuilt its website in 2026. On a phone it now loads in 1.1 seconds, down from 5.9, and it went live on 8 November 2026. I still look after it.

At a glance

The short version

Client: Kōhī by Ifuku, 44 Stanley, Milpark

Pages: a home page, and a gallery of 17 photographs and 20 short films

Phone load time: 1.1 seconds, down from 5.9

Images: 316 MB of source photography down to 3.9 MB on the site

Home page weight: about 141 KB, with no third-party requests

Built with: HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no build step

Open the site (opens in a new tab)

The problem

Slow, on the one screen that mattered

People look up a café on their phone, usually standing outside or on the way there, and they want the hours, the menu and directions. The previous version took 5.9 seconds to load on a phone. It pulled its fonts and a review widget from other companies' servers, and its images were far heavier than anything a phone screen can show.

Design

Built from the room itself

The colours are sampled from the café: the near-black of a record sleeve, the warm off-white of the plaster walls, the wood of the tables, and the indigo of the curtain over the counter. The type pairs Fraunces for headings with Inter for everything else, on a numbered editorial grid that folds down to a single column on a phone.

The top of the home page is a still photograph, on purpose. Every clip in their library is fast-cut social footage, and none of it loops without a visible jump. A still is the honest choice until there is a steady, ten-second shot to use.

Speed

How it got from 5.9 seconds to 1.1

Every image is WebP, in several sizes, so a phone downloads the small one and a laptop the large one. That took the photography from 316 MB to 3.9 MB. The fonts are hosted on the site itself and cut down to the characters it uses. No video loads until someone taps to play it. Nothing is fetched from another company's server, apart from the map, which only loads when you scroll to it.

The home page now weighs about 141 KB in total.

Access

Usable by everyone who visits

It has a skip link, visible focus outlines, a clean heading order, and text that meets WCAG AA contrast. The menu works from the keyboard and closes on Escape. The photo viewer uses the browser's own dialog, so focus behaves properly. The moving parts (the scroll reveals, the marquee and the spinning record) switch off for anyone whose device asks for reduced motion. Every page works with JavaScript turned off.
