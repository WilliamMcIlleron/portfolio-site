Instagram is where they check you out. It isn't where they find you.

Your feed does a real job, and it does it well — it convinces someone who already knows your name. But nobody opens Instagram to search "microneedling near me". They open Google, and whoever turns up there gets the booking.

Three things a feed can't do

None of this is an argument for posting less. It's the work Instagram was never built to do.

  1. Turn up in a search

    Someone new to the area types "facial" and your suburb. A Google listing on its own is thin; a page naming your treatments and where you are gives the search engine something to match.

  2. Answer "how much?" without a DM

    Plenty of people won't ask. Asking a price feels like starting a negotiation, so they quietly go to the studio that published one. A price list ends that.

  3. Stay findable

    A post is buried by Thursday. A page still says what you do, what it costs and how to book, two years from now, without you having to keep feeding it.

One page, doing the whole job

Everything someone needs in order to book you, on one screen they can scroll on their phone.

  • Your treatments, in your words

    What each one is, who it suits, how long it takes. Written so somebody who has never had it done knows whether to book it.

  • A price list

    Published, or a from-price if your treatments vary. Either way the question gets answered before it becomes a message you have to reply to.

  • Where you are, and parking

    A map, the actual entrance, and where to leave the car. Small details, but they're what a first-time client is nervous about.

  • A way to book

    A request form that emails you, or a button through to whatever booking system you already use. I'm not going to make you change it.

  • Your feed, still doing its job

    Instagram and WhatsApp linked from the page, so the people who want to look through your work before booking still can.

  • Fast on a bad connection

    It loads in about a second on phone data. A slow page loses the booking to the next studio in the results.

If you sell products too

Not every studio needs this. If you sell your own range, or retail someone else's, the page can take orders properly rather than turning every sale into a conversation in your inbox.

  • A real cart and checkout

    Sizes and variants, stock that counts down, discount codes, and an order confirmation that goes out without you typing it.

  • Card and instant EFT

    Through Payfast or Yoco, in rands, into your own account. Instant EFT matters here — plenty of people will pay from their banking app who won't type a card number into a shop they've just found.

  • Delivery you can actually charge for

    Flat rate, by area, or collection from the studio. Set once, applied at checkout, so you stop absorbing courier costs by accident.

  • You run it afterwards

    Adding a product, changing a price and marking something sold out are things you do from your phone. You don't come back to me for it.

R3,500 on top of the page — R5,500 for the two together. That covers loading your products, the photographs, the delivery rules, connecting the payment side, and testing a real transaction end to end before you go anywhere near a customer.

The shop itself is a subscription, and it's yours, not mine. It runs on Ecwid, which starts around $5 a month for up to ten products and about $35 a month up to a hundred. Your payment provider takes its cut on top — roughly 2% on instant EFT and around 3% on cards. I'd rather you know that now than find it after the site is built. Same principle as the domain: it's your account, so nothing is trapped with me.

If you only have a few products and don't need stock counts or variants, say so — a simpler version with a buy button per product costs less and has no monthly fee at all.
