    What you can hire me for

    Six jobs, one person. I build it, set it up and look after it, and you know what it costs before I start.

    1. 01

      Websites, built and run

      One site, built and then looked after. A single, well-built page is often the whole answer: if someone needs to find you, believe you and phone you, more pages is just more to keep current.

      1. You tell me what's wrong

        What the site needs to do, who it's for, and what you're working with now. A phone call or a long email, whichever you prefer. Free, and I'll tell you honestly if you don't need me.

      2. I quote a fixed price

        One number for the whole thing, not an hourly rate that drifts. If the scope changes we agree the change before I do the work.

      3. It goes live, and I keep it running

        You see the whole site on a real link before anything is public. After that, hosting, backups and the small changes you'll want every few weeks. You never have to learn a dashboard.

      Send a photo of what you do and I'll show you a mock-up (opens in a new tab). No charge, nothing agreed until you say so.

      R2,000 a single page

      R850 for each page after the first. Most sites land between R5,000 and R25,000.

      Upkeep from R800 a month, optional. The price depends on what it covers: hosting, backups and small changes for a one-page site is the starting point, and bigger sites and projects cost more.

      Domains aren't included: you buy the name, I point it at the site.

    2. 02

      Ads that bring the work in

      A website catches the people already looking for you by name. Ads reach the ones who haven't found you yet. I run Google search ads for local service businesses, pointed at a page built to turn a visit into a call or a WhatsApp. If your work photographs well (pools, kitchens, renovations, carports), I also make short ad videos from your own job photos for Facebook and Instagram.

      You pay Google and Meta directly from your own card, so the spend never passes through me and you can see every rand of it. If any part of a video is made with AI, I tell you which part. Each month you get a short report: what was spent, how many enquiries came in, and what each one cost.

      Quoted per business

      It depends on the channels and the content. If ads won't pay for themselves in your trade, I'll say so.

    3. 03

      Booking systems

      A way for customers to book themselves. The demo is a quiz funnel for a made-up pool company: a short quiz, a plan that fits the answers, then a day and a time. You see what the customer sees and what the owner gets.

      On a real site it connects to your Google Calendar or a free booking tool, so a time that's taken can't be booked twice.

      Try the booking demo →

      Quoted per business

      After I know what you use now, so I don't make you change it.

    4. 04

      Brands

      When your logo, colours and fonts look different on your site, Google profile, Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp, a customer wonders if it's the same business. There are three sizes of job.

      A fix when what you have doesn't match: a clean version of your logo with a one-page style sheet, then profiles that all look like one business. A brand system when you want it done properly: a logo set, colours, type, post templates, stationery and a guidelines document you keep.

      A full brand from scratch if you don't have one yet: I design the logo and everything around it, from the first sketches to the finished set, so the business looks like one thing from the first day.

      Quoted per business

      After I've looked at what you have, or talked through what you're starting from.

    5. 05

      Social media

      Posts for your own pages, made from your own photos and videos and planned a month at a time, so the feed stays active without you having to think about it every day.

      Quoted per business

      By the number of posts you want each month.

    6. 06

      Bigger, more technical builds

      Backends, integrations and automation: the part that has to be correct, not just look right. Silver Lakes runs on Paystack subaccounts with a server-side split a buyer can't edit in devtools, and every table is locked to its owner with row-level security. Faircopy, a demo, has real billing: webhooks, credit grants, quotas and a subscription flow. Vivarium's evolve loop calls Claude Code against its own codebase inside guardrails enforced in code, not asked for in a prompt.

      Scoped on a call

      The shape depends on what you already run and what it needs to talk to. If it's not a fit, I'll say so.
